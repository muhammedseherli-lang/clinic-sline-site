Sapphire FUE vs DHI: which hair transplant method suits you?

Medically reviewed by a licensed physician · Updated 2026-11-13

Key takeaways

  • Both methods take grafts with FUE. The difference is how the recipient area is prepared and how grafts are placed.
  • The right choice depends on your hair loss, donor area and goals, not on marketing.
  • Ask the clinic to explain why it recommends one method for you.

What they have in common

In both methods the doctor removes individual follicular units from the donor area, usually the back and sides of the scalp, and places them in thinning areas.

Sapphire FUE

Channels in the recipient area are opened with sapphire-tipped blades, and the grafts are then placed into them. Sapphire blades are finer than steel, and clinics use them to aim for dense, natural-looking placement and a comfortable recovery.

DHI

In DHI, grafts are loaded into an implanter pen and placed directly, which gives control over angle and depth. Clinics often use it for hairlines and for unshaven or partly shaven options.

How a doctor chooses

  • The area to cover and how dense it should be
  • The quality and quantity of your donor hair
  • Whether you want to keep hair long or avoid full shaving
  • Your plan for future hair loss

Sources

  • International Society of Hair Restoration Surgery (ISHRS)
  • MedlinePlus: Hair loss

Frequently asked questions

Is sapphire FUE better than DHI?

Neither is better for everyone. The choice depends on your case, and many clinics combine approaches. Ask for the reasoning behind the recommendation.

Can DHI be done without shaving?

Some clinics offer unshaven or partly shaven options, usually for limited areas. A doctor confirms whether it suits you.

Does the method change the price?

It can, along with graft count and surgery time. Ask for a written quote that names the technique.
